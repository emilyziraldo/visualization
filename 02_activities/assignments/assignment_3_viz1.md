Data visualization #1: Number of Tickets Issued in the City of Toronto by Year and Offence
Link to dataset: https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/police-annual-statistical-report-tickets-issued/

Q1. What software did you use to create your data visualization?
I used python software in a Jupyter notebook to create my visualization.

Q2. Who is your intended audience?
My intended audience is people who are interested in driving related offences in Toronto (e.g., government officials, citizens, police officers)

Q3. What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization?
My vizualization shows that the number of tickets given out for various types of traffic related offences increased between 2014 and 2024. 

Q4. What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots?
I tried to make it perceptual, aesthetic and substantive. 
Perceptual - by keeping it quite simple in design so the overall pattern of the data could be easily observed.
Aesthetic - I think it is pleasing to look at and easy to understand.
Substantive - The visualization is true to the data without any attempt to misconstrue the data or impact the viewers emotion or understanding (e.g., through the use of specific colours or directing focus to specific data). This is also done by including all available offences, rather than picking certain offences to focus on.

Q5. How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization?
I made my visualization in a Jupyter notebook using Python code. I also included a link to the data, which is open access and free to download. No data manipulation was done outside of the code in the Jupyter notebook. This means that anyone can go get the data, import it and re-run the code to create the figure.

Q6. How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?
I considered accessibility in my visualization by increasing the font size of the title and axis labels and also by using both different colours and markers to differentiate between offence types.

Q7. Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?
Stakeholders interested in driving offences in Toronto might be impacted by my visualization (e.g., citizens, police, tourists, government officials).

Q8. How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization?
I chose features based on what I thought would be interesting to the most people. I also like how this visualization used more of the data included in the dataset (E.g., all the years and all of the different types of offences).

Q9. What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?
I had to look up some of the function used in my visualization code and test the code multiple times before it worked properly.
