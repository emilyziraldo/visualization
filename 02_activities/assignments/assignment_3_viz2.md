Data visualization #2: 
Link to dataset: https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/toronto-raccoon-activity-index/

Q1. What software did you use to create your data visualization?
I used Excel to create my visualization.

Q2. Who is your intended audience?
My intended audience is people who are interested in racoon activity in different regions of Toronto (e.g., citizens, government officals, people moving to Toronto and considering where the best region to live in).

Q3. What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization?
My vizualization shows that the confidence level of racoons is similar across regions of Toronto. Note that confidence level is defined on the Toronto Open Data data webpage as being assessed based on racoon behaviour on a scale of 1-10 based on racoon behaviour.

Q4. What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots?
I tried to make it perceptual, aesthetic and substantive. 
Perceptual - I considered including other features of interest (e.g., the number of incidents of racoon-human standoff, the number of complaints_logged) but decided that adding additional axes and colours would increase the complexity of the figure and make it more difficult to descern the patterns for each variable. 
Aesthetic - I think it looks quite basic but still pleasing to look at and easy to understand. Also, the colours used for the bars (blue) contrast well with the error bars (black).
Substantive - The data is being presented without any inaccuracies or attempts to change the messaging. For example, the title ("Average Raccoon Confidence Level by Toronto Region") is  objective and lets the viewer come to their own conclusions about the message of the data.

Q5. How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization?
This visualization is not reproducible because it was done in Microsoft Excel. This might negatively impact the credibility of the visualization because it means that someone cannot make the exact same visualization unless they follow the same steps I followed when I created it. That being said, since the visualization is quite basic, I believe it could be reproduced quite easily by someone familiar with Excel.

Q6. How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?
I considered accessibility in my visualization by increasing the font size of the title and axis labels and also by no creating a graph that relies on distingishing between different colours. For example, since all the bars are the same colour, it is not conveying any additional information. I also chose blue since it is the colour often distinguishable for most types of colour vision deficiencies. 

Q7. Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?
I think people living in Toronto generally would be interested in this visualization. It is quite a fun and playful statistic (racoon confidence) but also relevant for people who are concerned about racoons in the regions they live in!

Q8. How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization?
I chose racoon confidence because I think it is an interesting and novel way to assess racoon activity because it reflects the behaviour of the racoons. This is opposition to more traditional metrics like racoon spottings, which don't give information about behaviour, which is more concerning rather than just seeing a racoon.

Q9. What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?
The data was originally presented by city wards. However, I thought that was too detailed and the large number of wards would be overwhelming and distracting, therefore I re-categorized the wards into regions.
